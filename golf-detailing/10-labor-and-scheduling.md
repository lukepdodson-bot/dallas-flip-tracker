# Labor, Hiring & Scheduling — Caddy 19

*Dallas–Fort Worth premium mobile detailing at golf/country clubs. All figures are 2026 estimates — verify current pricing before signing any contract.*

Luke's read is correct: **labor is the hardest part of this business.** Everything else — booking a club, marketing to members, setting prices — is a solved problem you can copy from other service businesses. Labor is different because the "product" walks out the door in the hands of someone you're not standing next to, working on a car that costs more than most people's annual salary, parked on private club property where one bad interaction can end the account. This document covers (A) how to hire and pay people you can trust with that, and (B) the scheduling/shift-signup tool Luke specifically asked for.

---

## PART A — LABOR MODEL & HIRING

### 1. The core challenge, stated plainly

At most detailing shops, a bad day means a customer complains and you offer a redo. At **Caddy 19**, a bad day can mean:

- A tech scratches a member's $150K car, and the member is a decision-maker at the club or knows the GM socially.
- A tech is rude, sloppy, or unprofessional on club grounds while members are walking to the first tee — the club's reputation is on the line, not just yours.
- A tech is unsupervised with member key fobs and vehicle access for 60–90 minutes per car.
- Quality varies day to day, and "premium" pricing stops being believable.

This means the hiring bar isn't "can this person clean a car." It's **reliability, trustworthiness, and consistency under zero supervision at a premium, reputation-sensitive location.** Every policy below (background checks, photo documentation, paid trials, QC checklists) exists to make that risk manageable — not to be bureaucratic for its own sake. Treat trust infrastructure as a cost of doing business at clubs, not overhead to cut once things get busy.

### 2. Staffing plan by phase

The golf-day rhythm matters: demand is heavily weighted to weekend mornings (members play, cars sit in the lot 3–5 hours) and lighter on weekday mornings/afternoons (corporate outings, senior weekday leagues). Staff the peak, not the average.

| Phase | Trigger | Staffing | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Prove the standard** | Pre-launch through first 1–2 clubs | Luke (or one lead tech) does every detail personally | You cannot train quality control you haven't personally defined. Luke needs to know exactly what a $X Full Round detail looks like, how long it takes, and where the pitfalls are before handing it off. |
| **Phase 2 — First hires** | Once a club is signed and volume exceeds 1 person's capacity on a Saturday | 1–2 part-time techs, working alongside Luke | Techs learn by shadowing on real cars at the real club. Luke is still present most Saturdays for QC and to manage the club relationship in person. |
| **Phase 3 — Small crew** | 2+ clubs, or weekend volume that needs 3+ techs simultaneously | Small crew (4–8 part-time techs) + one **lead tech** who trains new hires, runs QC checks, and can run a club solo when Luke isn't there | Luke shifts from doing details to running the business — sales, club relationships, hiring. The lead tech is the quality backstop; this role should be filled by your best, longest-tenured tech and paid a premium for it. |

**Scheduling implication of the golf-day rhythm:** most techs will want (and need) to be part-time, available primarily Thursday–Sunday mornings. Budget for a roster larger than your average daily need, because weekend mornings will require most of the crew at once while weekdays need a skeleton crew or none. This is exactly why the shift-signup tool in Part B matters — a rigid fixed schedule doesn't fit this demand curve.

### 3. Pay-structure options

| Structure | How it works | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **W-2 hourly** ($16–22/hr + tips) | Fixed hourly wage regardless of cars completed | Predictable for techs (easier to hire/retain); simplest to run payroll and comply with labor law; full control over how/when/where work is done | Labor cost doesn't flex with revenue; slow techs cost the same as fast ones; no built-in incentive for speed or upsell |
| **Per-car commission** (~$40–60/Full Round, less for Express/Interior-only) | Tech paid per completed job, not per hour | Strong incentive for speed + quality (repeat business, tips, referrals for the tech); labor cost scales directly with revenue — you don't pay for slow weekdays | Techs may rush and cut corners on a premium product where corner-cutting is exactly the risk; harder to require attendance at slow times, meetings, training; complicates W-2 minimum-wage math (still must equal minimum wage per hour worked) |
| **Percentage of ticket** (~30–40%) | Tech paid a % of what that job billed | Similar incentive alignment to commission; scales naturally with premium pricing tiers (higher-ticket jobs pay more) | Same speed/quality tension as commission; ticket price is set by the business, so the tech has less visibility/control than flat per-car pay, which can feel opaque |

**Recommendation:** **W-2 hourly base, with a per-car or per-ticket bonus layered on top**, not pure commission. Concretely: pay $16–20/hr as the guaranteed floor, plus a $10–20 bonus per completed Full Round (scaled down for Express jobs) once the tech is fully trained and off probation. This gets you:

- **Legal/practical control**: because these are scheduled shifts at a specific club, at a specific time, using company processes and often company supplies — that's the fact pattern of an employee, not a contractor (see #4). W-2 hourly is the compliant default.
- **Incentive alignment without the corner-cutting risk of pure commission**: the bonus rewards speed and volume, but the hourly floor means a tech isn't financially punished for taking the extra 5 minutes to get the wheel wells right, which matters enormously with member reputational risk on the line.
- **Cost predictability during ramp-up**: pure commission only works once volume is steady and techs can count on filling their day; early on, guaranteed hourly keeps good people from walking when a club's volume is inconsistent.

As volume and confidence in the team grow, the bonus percentage can increase and the hourly floor can be treated more like a backstop — but starting hourly-first, bonus-second is the lower-risk path for both classification and quality.

### 4. W-2 vs. 1099 — read this before you decide

Because Caddy 19 controls **when** techs work (specific shifts), **where** they work (assigned club and time), **how** the work is done (quality checklist, uniform, company process), and **quality-checks the output**, this looks like classic employee (W-2) territory, not independent-contractor (1099) territory, under both IRS common-law rules and Texas Workforce Commission guidance. Misclassifying scheduled, quality-controlled shift workers as 1099 contractors is one of the most commonly audited/penalized practices for small service businesses — back payroll taxes, penalties, and potential unemployment-insurance liability are all real exposure.

**This is not legal or tax advice, and the final call is Luke's to make with a bookkeeper or employment attorney before the first hire is paid.** The scheduling tool in Part B (assigned shifts, specific clubs, specific times) will itself generate a paper trail that supports W-2 classification — worth keeping in mind either way.

### 5. Where to recruit

- **Indeed / Facebook local groups** — post in DFW-area detailing, car-enthusiast, and gig-work Facebook groups; Indeed for broader reach and to catch career-changers.
- **Poach skilled hands from car washes and existing detail shops** — these people already have the hand skills and know the difference between a $20 wash and a $200 detail; often underpaid and undervalued where they are.
- **Referral bonus** — pay existing techs $50–100 for a referral who completes 90 days; your best techs know other people who take pride in the work, and a referred hire is pre-vetted socially.
- **Trade/vocational schools** — auto detailing, automotive tech, or even cosmetology programs (attention to fine detail, working with clients) can be a surprisingly good source of trainable candidates.

### 6. Screening & onboarding

1. **Background check** — non-negotiable. Techs handle member key fobs and unattended access to six-figure vehicles on private property; this is the one control that isn't optional.
2. **Paid working interview / trial detail** — bring the candidate in for one real (paid) detail on a company or friendly-customer car, supervised. This reveals actual skill and work habits far better than an interview.
3. **Training checklist** — a written, step-by-step process for each service tier (Express, Full Round, add-ons) so quality doesn't depend on who happened to train the person.
4. **Quality standards + per-car checklist** — a short physical or app-based checklist the tech (or lead) signs off on per car: exterior wash/dry, wheels/tires, interior vacuum, dash/console wipe-down, windows, final walk-around. Keep it fast (2 minutes) — the point is consistency, not paperwork.
5. **Uniform** — branded shirt/hat at minimum. Signals professionalism to members and makes techs identifiable on club grounds (security and trust both benefit).

### 7. Trust & risk controls

- **Key handling protocol** — keys are checked out and back in by name/time (even a simple sign-out sheet or app log); never left in an unlocked cart or bag; single point of accountability if something goes missing.
- **Before/after photos on every car** — timestamped photos of the vehicle on arrival and completion, stored per job. This protects the tech (proof of pre-existing damage) and protects Caddy 19 and the club (proof nothing was damaged during service). Non-negotiable for premium/high-value vehicles.
- **Damage-claim process** — a clear, written procedure: tech reports immediately, photos pulled, business insurance notified, member contacted by Luke directly (never left to the tech). Speed and transparency here are what preserve a club relationship after an incident.
- **Bonding / insurance** — carry general liability and consider garage-keepers or bailee coverage (you have custody of customers' property); ask your insurance broker specifically about coverage for detailing/mobile service on client-owned vehicles. Confirm requirements with each club — many will require a certificate of insurance naming the club as additional insured before granting access.
- **Conduct standards on club property** — no phone use except for job-related photos/logging, no radio/music audible to members, professional dress and language at all times, techs briefed that they are guests representing both Caddy 19 and the club to its members.

## PART B — SCHEDULING & JOB-SIGNUP TOOL

### 8. What Luke actually needs the tool to do

Distilled from his ask:

- **Workers set their own availability** — the days/time windows they're generally able to work.
- **Admin (Luke) posts upcoming bookings/shifts** tied to a specific club and date/time.
- **Workers browse open jobs/shifts and claim (sign up for) them** — self-service, not Luke manually calling around.
- **Admin sees coverage at a glance** — which shifts are filled, which are open, who's assigned where — and can manually assign or reassign as needed.
- **Reminders/notifications** so techs don't forget a shift they claimed, and Luke gets alerted if a shift is going unfilled close to the date.

This is a fairly standard "shift marketplace" pattern — availability + open-shift self-signup — which several off-the-shelf small-business scheduling apps already do well.

### 9. Build vs. buy

| | Off-the-shelf app | Custom lightweight web app |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost to start** | Free–$100/mo depending on crew size and plan | Real build cost (developer time or Luke's own time), even for an MVP |
| **Time to launch** | Same day — sign up, add employees, post shifts | Days to a few weeks for a functional MVP |
| **Fit to "club-based jobs"** | Generic "shift" model; club = a text field or tag, not a first-class concept | Purpose-built: clubs, bookings, and jobs can be modeled exactly as the business works |
| **Availability + open-shift claim** | Built-in on most mid-tier plans (see table below) | Has to be built from scratch |
| **Branding** | Third-party app, not Caddy 19-branded | Fully Caddy 19 branded, could double as a recruiting/professionalism signal |
| **Maintenance** | None — vendor's problem | Luke's (or a developer's) problem indefinitely |
| **Right for** | Launch through small crew (roughly up to ~10–15 techs) | Once crew size, club count, or booking complexity outgrows what a generic scheduling app models well |

**Off-the-shelf comparison for launch:**

| App | Approx. pricing (2026) | Availability feature | Open-shift self-signup | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Homebase** | Free tier for 1 location / up to ~20 employees; paid tiers roughly $20–100+/mo as features (advanced scheduling, HR, payroll) are added | Yes — employees set availability, request time off, managers approve | Yes — open shifts can be posted and claimed on paid tiers | Best-known free tier; started as a scheduling tool for hourly retail/restaurant work, which maps well onto shift-based detailing crews |
| **When I Work** | ~$2.50–$8/user/month depending on tier (add-ons like time & attendance push it higher) | Yes | Yes, on Pro tier and above | Strong scheduling-first tool; per-user pricing scales predictably as crew grows |
| **Connecteam** | Free "Small Business" plan for up to 10 users; paid tiers roughly $35–$119/month (per hub, not per user) above that | Yes | Yes — open shift posting is a core scheduling feature; open-shift *approval* workflow on the Advanced tier | Free-for-10 makes it attractive at Caddy 19's Phase 2 crew size; flat per-hub pricing (not per-user) is good once the crew grows past a handful |
| **Deputy** | Roughly $1.50–$6.50/user/month depending on source and tier | Yes | Yes | Geared slightly more toward larger, multi-location teams; likely more capability than Caddy 19 needs at launch |

**Recommendation for launch: start with Homebase's free tier or Connecteam's free-up-to-10 plan.** Both already do exactly what Luke described — techs set availability, admin posts shifts, techs claim open shifts, admin sees coverage — at $0/month for the Phase 2 crew size (1–2 part-time techs, maybe growing to 4–6). Connecteam's free tier covers up to 10 users and includes basic scheduling; Homebase's free tier is capped tighter on features but is the most scheduling-native of the group. Either is a same-day setup with zero build cost. Move to a paid tier of whichever one is chosen once the crew or club count outgrows the free plan's limits — that's still far cheaper and faster than building custom.

**When to build custom:** once Caddy 19 has enough clubs and crew that "club" needs to be a real object in the system (not a text tag) — e.g., club-specific instructions, gate codes, contact info, recurring club-day templates, tech ratings per club — and once the volume justifies a few thousand dollars and real development time. At that point a purpose-built, Caddy 19-branded tool pays for itself in time saved and professionalism. This repository could host that MVP when the time comes; the spec below is written so it's ready to build without re-deriving the requirements.

### 10. Spec for the eventual custom tool

**Core entities**

| Entity | Key fields |
|---|---|
| **Worker** | name, contact, hire date, status (active/probation/inactive), pay rate, home base/travel radius |
| **Club** | name, address, gate/access notes, primary club contact, standing instructions |
| **Booking / Job** | club, member/customer, service tier (Express/Full Round/etc.), date, arrival window, vehicle info, status (unassigned/assigned/in-progress/complete) |
| **Shift** | linked to one or more Bookings at a Club on a given date/time block, capacity (how many techs needed), assigned Worker(s), status (open/claimed/confirmed/completed/no-show) |
| **Availability** | Worker, day-of-week + time-window recurring pattern, plus date-specific overrides (blackout dates, one-off extra availability) |

**Key screens**

*Worker view:*
- Set/edit weekly availability (recurring pattern + one-off overrides)
- Browse open shifts (filterable by club, date, distance) and claim one
- "My Schedule" — upcoming confirmed shifts, with club address/instructions and a way to message the admin or flag an issue

*Admin (Luke) view:*
- Post a new shift/job — pick club, date, time window, service tier(s), number of techs needed
- Coverage dashboard — calendar or list view showing filled vs. open shifts at a glance, color-coded by urgency (e.g., red if unfilled within 48 hours of the shift)
- Assign/reassign a worker manually (override self-signup when needed)
- Worker directory with availability, reliability history (no-shows, ratings), and pay rate

**Must-have rules**

- **No double-booking** — a worker cannot claim two shifts that overlap in time, and cannot claim a shift outside their stated availability without an explicit override.
- **Confirmation step** — claiming a shift should require a confirm action (and ideally a reminder 24h and 2h before) so "claimed" doesn't silently become a no-show.
- **Cutoff times** — shifts can only be claimed/dropped up until some cutoff before the job (e.g., 24 hours out); after that, dropping a shift requires notifying the admin directly, not just self-removing from the app, so Luke isn't blindsided the morning of a club day.
- **Audit trail** — every claim, assignment, and cancellation timestamped and attributed — this doubles as the W-2 shift-control documentation referenced in Part A, Section 4.
