# Caddy 19 — Use-of-Funds Budget Breakdown

*Dallas–Fort Worth mobile golf-club detailing. All figures are 2026 estimates for planning purposes, not quotes or commitments. Rig/equipment capex is detailed in full in a dedicated capex doc; totals are referenced here for use-of-funds context.*

---

## 1. Purpose and Two Funding Scenarios

This document answers one question: **where does the first dollar of startup capital go, and how much is enough?** Two scenarios are presented because the honest answer depends on how fast the owner wants to scale.

### Scenario A — Bootstrap Launch (~$4,000–$6,000 all-in)

**Assumptions:**
- Owner uses an **existing personal vehicle** (SUV/truck/van) fitted with a portable rig — no wrap, no dedicated trailer.
- Owner is the sole technician; no hired labor in this scenario.
- Water and power sourced from the club's spigots/outlets where possible, supplemented by a portable tank and battery/generator.
- Goal: get to a signed pilot club and first paying Clubhouse/Members' subscribers with minimum cash at risk, then reinvest revenue into the proper rig (Scenario B) once volume proves out.
- Marketing is founder-led (in-person pro shop visits, one pilot club, word of mouth) rather than paid.

### Scenario B — Funded Launch (~$25,000–$35,000)

**Assumptions:**
- Owner commits to a **proper wrapped rig** (truck or enclosed trailer, fully branded, professional-grade water reclamation and equipment) from day one — see the dedicated rig/capex doc for the full $12k–$22k build-out.
- Budget includes 3–6 months of operating runway (insurance, software, supplies, marketing) so the business isn't cash-starved while club relationships and membership base build.
- Budget includes a **first hire** (part-time technician or apprentice) to run a second rig or cover a second club day, once volume supports it — full labor economics live in the labor/staffing doc; this document references the line only.
- Positioning is fully premium from launch: professional wrap, uniforms, multi-club presence.

Both scenarios assume the brand/logo work is already complete (per the brand & positioning doc) and that pricing follows the tiers established in the business model doc: **The Turn** (~$85 avg), **The Full Round** (~$210 avg, the hero service), and **The Championship** (~$400 avg), plus recurring membership plans (**Clubhouse** $99/mo, **Members'** $159/mo, **Championship** $279/mo).

## 4. Break-Even Analysis

**Contribution per Full Round ticket: ~$145** (after ~$12 supplies cost, on a ~$210 average ticket, ~65% gross margin). Using the Full Round as the baseline unit since it's the hero service most volume clusters around.

**Step 1 — Fixed costs to cover (owner-operator, lean, no hire):**
Using the midpoint of the lean monthly total: **~$960/month** in fixed operating costs (insurance, software, fuel, phone, marketing, misc — excluding consumables, which are already netted out of the $145 contribution figure).

**Step 2 — Details needed per month to break even:**

$960 ÷ $145 contribution per detail ≈ **6.6 details/month**

→ **Round up: ~7 Full Round-equivalent details per month covers fixed costs.**

**Step 3 — Translate to club-days per week:**

One tech working a prime golf day realistically completes ~2 Full Rounds + 2 Turns, generating **$520–$680/day** in revenue and roughly **2 Full Round-equivalents worth of contribution** (Turns contribute less individually but still add positive margin — call it a blended ~$300–$350 contribution per club day when mixing Turns and Full Rounds).

$960 ÷ ~$325 blended contribution/day ≈ **~3 club-days/month**

→ **Roughly one club-day per week covers fixed costs.** Everything beyond that — additional club days, memberships, Championship-tier tickets — is profit contribution toward owner income.

**Step 4 — Path to a target owner income (illustrative, e.g. $5,000/month owner draw):**

Fixed costs + target draw = $960 + $5,000 = $5,960/month needed in contribution

$5,960 ÷ $145 ≈ **41 Full Round-equivalent details/month**

At ~4 details captured per club day (2 Full Rounds + 2 Turns, blended), that's roughly **10 club-days/month, or ~2–3 club days per week** — well within a single tech's capacity, and squarely why the model targets prime tee-time slots at 2–3 partner clubs rather than one club alone. Recurring memberships (Clubhouse/Members'/Championship) compress this further by locking in predictable monthly contribution before a single walk-up ticket is sold.

## 6. Where the Money Should Go First (Priority Order)

Ranked by what most directly moves the business toward a **signed pilot club and first paying members** — not by convenience or excitement.

1. **Insurance / Certificate of Insurance (COI).** No club will sign a pilot agreement without proof of GL and garagekeepers/care-custody-control coverage. This unlocks every other conversation — fund it first, even before equipment purchases are finalized.
2. **Licenses/permits (TX sales tax permit, DBA, any city permit).** Free-to-cheap and required to legally invoice and collect payment. Do this in the same week as insurance.
3. **Booking/payments software setup.** A club won't take the pitch seriously without a way for members to book and pay instantly. This is the cheapest line in the whole budget relative to its impact — prioritize it over any physical gear beyond the basics.
4. **Core detailing equipment + water-reclamation/turf-safe gear.** The minimum kit needed to actually perform a Full Round on-site and satisfy a club's runoff/turf-safety questions. This is the functional floor — can't deliver the service without it.
5. **Initial supplies/consumables.** Stock enough for the first few weeks of tickets; restock out of revenue after that.
6. **Working capital / cash buffer.** Keep a reserve to smooth the gap between spending on the above and the first real revenue landing.
7. **Brand & print basics (cards, bag tags/magnets, minimal signage) and a simple website.** Needed to look credible walking into a pro shop, but a one-page site and a stack of cards beats an expensive build — don't over-invest here pre-launch.
8. **Uniforms.** A clean polo/hat matters for the first impression at the club but can start minimal (1–2 sets) and expand later.
9. **Rig wrap and dedicated trailer/truck build-out.** The single biggest capex line and the one to defer longest in Bootstrap Scenario A — reinvest into this once the pilot club is signed and membership revenue is proving the model. In Funded Scenario B this moves earlier by design, but even then it should follow, not precede, the insurance/booking/permit foundation above.
10. **First hire.** Last on the list regardless of scenario — only justified once club-day demand consistently exceeds one technician's capacity (see the labor/staffing doc for the trigger metrics and pay structure).

**Bottom line:** the first ~$1,000–$1,500 of any budget — bootstrap or funded — should go to insurance, permits, and booking software before a single polisher or wrap is purchased. That's what turns a pitch into a signed pilot club.
