# 11 — Economics: One Club vs. Scaled Across DFW

All figures are 2026 estimates and clearly labeled assumptions. The companion spreadsheet
`Caddy19-economics-model.xlsx` has every driver as an editable cell — change one and both
scenarios recompute. Vehicle costs are modeled as **leases** (per Luke's plan), which keeps
the vehicle out of startup capital and into monthly opex.

**Grounding (2026):** commercial cargo-van lease ≈ $450–1,300/mo (ProMaster/Transit ~$650
at 36 mo); detailing GL insurance ~$54/mo avg, garagekeepers ~$38/mo, BOP ~$89/mo;
commercial auto ~$200–250/mo per van; workers' comp required for W-2 physical labor.

## Scenario B — Scaled across DFW's private clubs

**Setup:** ~**10 clubs**, **5 rigs/crews** (each crew covers ~2 clubs across the week),
**10 techs**, **1 operations manager** running day-to-day, Luke as owner (stepped back).
Per-club revenue modeled slightly lower than the single-club best case ($9,500 vs.
$10,140) to be conservative across a portfolio at different maturities.

1–2 months as demand proves out. External capital only accelerates the timeline.

## Costs people forget (and that I've built in or flagged)

**Built into the models above:**
- **Payroll taxes + workers' comp** (14% burden) — workers' comp matters; techs do
  physical labor and handle expensive cars.
- **Credit-card processing (~2.9%)** — on $1.14M that's ~$33k/yr. Easy to overlook.
- **Club revenue share (12%)** — the cost of the partnership that makes the whole thing work.
- **Equipment replacement reserve** — gear wears out; budget for it, don't get surprised.

**Not in the monthly model — flagged so they don't bite you:**
- **☔ Weather (the #1 operational risk).** This is outdoor work. Rain/ice = a lost
  club-day with wages still owed. DFW averages ~35–45 rain days/yr. *Mitigants:* flexible
  same-week rescheduling, canopies for light weather, and — most importantly — **monthly
  memberships that bill regardless of any single day's weather.** Model a ~5–8% revenue
  haircut for weather until you have data.
- **Seasonality.** DFW golf is close to year-round but softens Dec–Feb. Expect lighter
  Q4/Q1; the models are annualized averages.
- **No-shows / cancellations** — members forget or leave early. Confirmations + a
  cancellation window help.
- **TX franchise tax** (once revenue clears the threshold) and **annual filings** — your books/legal domain.
- **Recurring hiring costs** — background checks, uniforms/laundry, training time, turnover.
- **Customer/club acquisition** — free 30-day pilots mean early months at a club run
  below the mature run-rate.
- **Owner's salary** — both scenarios show cash flow *before* paying Luke; decide salary
  vs. distributions with your bookkeeper.
- **Bad-weather/damage claims deductibles** — even with garagekeepers coverage, you eat
  the deductible on a claim.

## Bottom line

| | One club | Scaled (~10 clubs) |
|--|--|--|
| Startup capital | ~$14k | ~$87k (fund organically) |
| Annual revenue | ~$122k | ~$1.14M |
| Owner pre-tax cash flow | ~$28k/yr | ~$275k/yr |
| Margin | ~23% | ~24% |
| Luke's role | Operator (hands-on) | Owner (manager runs it) |

One club proves it and pays a little. **The business case is the scale-up** — same margins,
10× the dollars, and you're out of the day-to-day. The sequence matters more than the
capital: land one club, over-deliver, reinvest, repeat.
