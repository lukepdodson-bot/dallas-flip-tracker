# Capex & Mobile Rig Gameplan — Birdie Detail Co.

**Market:** Dallas–Fort Worth | **Prepared:** July 2026 | **All figures are 2026 estimates**

This document is the buildout plan for the truck/trailer and equipment side of the business: what to buy, how to pay for it, and the order to do it in. It picks up where the startup checklist ([06-startup-checklist-and-costs.md](./06-startup-checklist-and-costs.md)) left off — that doc covers minimum cash-to-open; this one covers the "real rig" once the concept is proven and a club is on the books.

---

## 1. Rig Philosophy — Non-Negotiables

Golf and country clubs are not a normal residential/commercial driveway environment. Three requirements are hard gates, not nice-to-haves, because losing them loses the club relationship (and possibly a hole):

1. **Fully self-contained.** The rig carries its own fresh water and its own power. We never tap a club's spigots, outlets, or irrigation system, and we never ask staff for anything mid-service. This is what makes us "invisible" to operations and easy for a GM to say yes to.
2. **100% runoff reclamation.** Every drop of wash/rinse water is captured and hauled off-site — nothing touches turf, cart paths, greens drainage, or storm drains. This is the single biggest liability and compliance issue for on-course detailing (chemical runoff into irrigation ponds or storm systems is the #1 reason a club would shut a vendor down). A reclamation mat/berm + wet-vac recovery system is mandatory from day one, not a Phase 2 upgrade.
3. **Quiet operation.** Members are playing golf 20 feet away. A screaming gas generator or a straight-piped pressure washer engine during someone's backswing is a fast way to get banned. This pushes us toward battery/inverter power and low-dB equipment wherever the budget allows, and toward generator placement/timing discipline where it doesn't.

Everything below is filtered through these three constraints first, cost second.

## 2. Vehicle Options

| Option | Est. Used Cost (2026) | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Bootstrap** — existing vehicle + open utility trailer or truck-bed rig | ~$3,000–4,000 in gear, $0 vehicle (use what Luke already owns) | Fastest to revenue, near-zero capital risk, proves demand before committing real money, easy to reverse if the concept doesn't work | Exposed to weather, less "pro" curb appeal at a country club gate, limited water/gear capacity, more load/unload labor per job, no secure overnight storage of gear |
| **(b) Used cargo van** (Ford Transit, RAM ProMaster, Chevy Express) | ~$15,000–28,000 | All-in-one — water, power, and gear live permanently mounted inside; weatherproof; lockable/secure; single vehicle to insure, plate, and park; best "professional operator" impression at a gate | Higher upfront capital; a mechanical problem takes the whole business off-road; converted interior (tank mounts, shelving, power) is itself a build cost on top of the van; less flexible if you ever want to separate "daily driver" from "work rig" |
| **(c) Used pickup + enclosed cargo trailer** | ~$12,000–22,000 combined | Trailer stays fully stocked and can be dropped/left secured while the truck is used for other things; truck can double as a personal/second vehicle; trailer is a rolling billboard; easier to add a second trailer later without buying a second van; if the tow vehicle breaks, a spare truck can hitch up and keep the business running | Two vehicles to insure/maintain; requires trailer parking/storage; towing adds fuel cost and driving complexity; less weatherproof than a van interior unless the trailer is well-sealed |

### Recommendation

**Start bootstrap (a)** to prove demand with the pilot club at near-zero capital risk — this matches the "low cost now" mandate and validates the model before real money is committed. **Once a pilot club is signed** with a recurring cadence (not just a one-off event), step up to **(c) used pickup + enclosed cargo trailer** as the primary next move:

- Lower capital outlay than a van for comparable capability.
- The trailer *is* the rig — it stays loaded and turf-ready, so setup/teardown at the club is faster than reloading truck-bed gear each trip.
- Preserves optionality: if volume later justifies a second crew, buy a second trailer before buying a second van, which is the cheaper way to scale.
- A cargo van (b) becomes the better choice only if Luke specifically wants a single locked, weatherproof, always-ready vehicle and is fine with the higher entry cost — worth revisiting at Phase 3 if volume supports two full-time rigs.

## 3. Itemized Equipment Build

"Lean" = adequate, reliable, gets the job done at pro-detail quality. "Pro" = faster, quieter, more durable, and scales better under daily commercial use. Buy lean at Phase 1–2; upgrade line-by-line as jobs justify it rather than re-buying the whole kit.

| Item | Purpose | Lean ($) | Pro ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure washer | Rinse/pre-soak, wheel wells, undercarriage | 250 | 900 (gas, low-dB commercial cart unit) |
| Portable water tank (35–65 gal) | Onboard fresh water so the club never has to supply a spigot | 250 | 550 (65 gal + baffled/slosh-resistant + mounting) |
| Power — generator or battery/inverter | Runs vacuum, polisher, extractor, pump | 400 (small inverter generator, quiet-rated) | 1,800 (lithium battery/inverter power station — silent, no exhaust, club-friendly) |
| Hot-water option (later add-on) | Better degreasing/carpet extraction results | — (skip at Phase 1) | 1,200 (inline propane water heater unit) |
| Wet/dry shop vac | Interior vacuuming + reclamation-mat water recovery | 150 | 450 (commercial-grade, higher CFM, quieter) |
| Carpet/upholstery extractor | Interior deep-clean upsell service | 300 | 900 (pro extractor, faster dry time) |
| Dual-action polisher(s) | Paint correction, swirl removal, upsell tier | 150 (1 unit) | 500 (2 units — random-orbit + rotary for correction jobs) |
| Steamer (optional) | Interior sanitizing, tight-detail upsell | — (skip) | 350 |
| Chemicals/consumables starter kit | Soap, wax/sealant, APC, tire shine, glass, interior cleaner | 300 | 600 (better-margin/longer-lasting pro-line products) |
| Microfiber inventory | Wash mitts, drying towels, applicator pads, buffing towels | 150 | 350 (larger rotation, color-coded to avoid cross-contamination) |
| Water-reclamation mat/berm + wet-vac recovery | **Mandatory** — 100% runoff capture, turf/storm-drain protection | 600 (portable containment mat/berm kit) | 1,400 (larger commercial containment mat + dedicated recovery pump) |
| Pop-up canopy | Shade for polishing work, weather cover, branding surface | 150 | 350 (heavy-duty, printed with logo) |
| A-frame sign | On-site visibility, curiosity-driver for other members | 80 | 180 (weighted, weatherproof, branded) |
| Storage/shelving | Organized, secure gear storage in trailer/truck bed | 150 | 500 (built-in van/trailer shelving system) |
| First-aid/safety kit | Basic first aid, fire extinguisher, chemical gloves/eyewear | 60 | 120 (upgraded kit + trailer-mounted fire suppression) |
| **Subtotal (equipment only, excludes vehicle)** | | **~$2,840** | **~$10,150** |

Notes:
- The lean column is close to the $3–4k bootstrap gear estimate in Section 2(a) — this is intentional; Phase 1 buys almost entirely from the lean column.
- The reclamation mat/berm line is the one item where "lean" should not mean "skip" — this is the club-access gate, not a discretionary upgrade.
- Power is the other line worth stretching for early if cash allows: a quiet battery/inverter setup is a real differentiator when pitching a club GM ("no generator noise near the greens").

## 4. Buy vs. Lease vs. Finance

**Recommendation: buy used, pay cash, at every phase covered in this plan.** Reasoning:

- Keeps the business debt-free and lean while demand is still being proven — matches the owner's stated cost posture.
- Used pressure washers, vacs, extractors, and polishers are a mature secondhand market (contractor liquidations, detailing shop upgrades) — real savings with minimal quality risk if inspected/tested before purchase.
- A cash-bought rig has no monthly payment pressure, which matters for a seasonal, weather-dependent, still-growing revenue base.
- No loan underwriting, personal guarantee, or interest cost while the business is still establishing a track record.

**Leasing/financing does exist as an option** — equipment financing, a business line of credit, or an SBA-backed loan could make sense once there's revenue history and Luke wants to accelerate the Phase 2/3 buildout faster than cash flow allows, or to preserve cash for marketing/hiring instead of tying it up in a truck. **That tradeoff is Luke's call to make with his bookkeeper/accountant** — this plan does not recommend a specific lender, structure, or loan product. The default path here assumes cash purchase of used equipment throughout.

## 5. Vehicle Wrap / Branding

| Option | Cost Range (2026) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetic door signs | ~$80–200 | Removable, reusable across vehicles, zero commitment — cheapest way to look legitimate immediately |
| Partial wrap (light — logo + accent panels) | $500–1,500 | Good middle step once a dedicated work vehicle/trailer is in regular use |
| Full wrap | $2,500–4,000 | Best presence and durability; makes sense once the rig is a permanent, dedicated asset (not the daily driver) and driving frequency justifies the "rolling billboard" value |

**Recommendation:** Start with **magnets** on whatever vehicle is bootstrapping the pilot (Phase 1) — cheap, reversible, and enough to look credible pulling into a club lot. **Move to a wrap after the first signed club** and once the truck/trailer from Phase 2 is a dedicated, non-personal work vehicle — at that point a partial wrap is the reasonable default, with a full wrap justified once volume/second-club growth (Phase 3) makes the visibility payoff clearly worth it.

## 6. Phased Capex Roadmap

| Phase | Trigger to Enter | What's Built | Rough Capital Needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Phase 1 — Prove Demand** | Business ready to take first paying jobs | Bootstrap rig: existing vehicle + open trailer or truck bed, lean equipment tier, magnetic signage, mandatory reclamation mat | ~$3,000–4,200 |
| **Phase 2 — First Signed Club** | A club signs a recurring on-site agreement (not a one-off event) | Step up to used pickup + enclosed cargo trailer (or cargo van), upgrade equipment toward the pro tier (power + water tank first), partial vehicle wrap | ~$15,000–28,000 (vehicle/trailer) + ~$3,000–5,000 (equipment upgrades) |
| **Phase 3 — Second Rig & Tech** | Volume across 2+ clubs consistently exceeds single-crew capacity | Second trailer or van build-out, hire/train a second detailer, add scheduling/booking software and possibly a second driver, consider full wrap on primary rig | ~$18,000–30,000 (second rig, equipment, and initial tech/software setup) |

Advancing a phase should be a demand decision, not a calendar decision — each step up in capital only happens once bookings justify the added capacity, not on a fixed timeline.

## 7. Maintenance & Consumable Cadence

| Category | Cadence | Rough Monthly Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Chemicals (soap, APC, wax/sealant, tire shine, glass, interior cleaner) | Restock every 2–4 weeks depending on job volume | $150–350 |
| Microfiber towels/pads/mitts | Rotate in fresh stock monthly; retire worn items | $30–60 |
| Pressure washer service (oil, seals, nozzles) | Inspect monthly; service every 3 months or ~100 hours | $20–40 avg (amortized) |
| Generator/battery power system | Gas gen: oil change every 50 hrs; battery system: no service, just charge cycle checks | $15–30 avg (gas only) |
| Vacuum/extractor filters & seals | Check monthly, replace filters quarterly | $15–25 |
| Water tank sanitation | Flush/sanitize tank monthly | $5–10 |
| Reclamation mat/berm inspection | Check for tears/leaks before every club visit; replace as needed | $10–20 avg (amortized) |
| Trailer/vehicle tires, brakes, hitch hardware | Inspect monthly, service per manufacturer interval | $20–40 avg (amortized) |
| Fuel/charging | Ongoing, scales with job count | $80–200 (varies with route density) |
| **Estimated total recurring consumables/maintenance** | | **~$350–750/month**, scaling up with job volume |

This cadence should be built into a simple pre/post-shift checklist so equipment failures never happen mid-job at a club — a breakdown on-site is a worse outcome than the cost of proactive maintenance.
