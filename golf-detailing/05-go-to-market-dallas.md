# Go-to-Market: Dallas–Fort Worth

**Fairway Detail Co. (working name)** — on-site mobile detailing for golf & country club members. Members drop keys at the bag drop, play their round, drive home in a clean car. Verify all club names, cities, membership figures, and contact details before outreach — club rosters, GM tenure, and management structures change.

---

## 1. GTM Thesis

We sell to **clubs, not consumers** — a club that says yes hands us its entire membership as a warm, aggregated customer base in one motion, which no amount of direct-to-consumer marketing can replicate. We win by **landing mid-tier, high-membership, family-oriented clubs and Invited/ClubCorp-portfolio properties first**, where GMs are incentivized to add member amenities and one relationship can cascade across multiple clubs, then use those wins as proof to **earn the ultra-exclusive trophy clubs later**, once we have a track record, testimonials, and operational polish they'll actually trust.

*Verify current ownership/management (ClubCorp was rebranded to Invited; confirm which DFW clubs remain in the portfolio), current GM names, and membership counts before outreach — these move.*

### Tier 2 — Expand (after 1–2 Tier-1 wins)

- Additional independent family/lifestyle clubs not yet contacted (canvas the broader Plano/Frisco/Southlake/McKinney corridor for comparable membership profiles).
- Upscale semi-private and daily-fee clubs with strong member/guest volume — these don't require the same "exclusive club" sales cycle but still have the target vehicle demographic.
- Any additional Invited/ClubCorp properties not covered in the Tier 1 push.
- Clubs where a Tier 1 GM or golf pro can make a warm introduction (this is the fastest path into Tier 2 — ask for referrals explicitly during the Tier 1 pilot).

### Tier 3 — Trophy Clubs (pursue later, once proof exists)

Preston Trail Golf Club, Dallas National Golf Club, Brook Hollow Golf Club, Vaquero Club, Maridoe Golf Club, Trinity Forest Golf Club.

These are prestigious, protective of their member experience, and have long, relationship-driven sales cycles — often requiring a member-sponsor introduction rather than a cold approach. Do not lead with these. Approach only after Fairway Detail Co. has 2–3 signed clubs, real testimonials, and (ideally) a warm introduction through an existing member or GM relationship. Verify current GM and membership chair contacts close to the time of outreach, since these roles turn over and access is gated tightly.

## 3. Founding Club Pilot Strategy

**Goal:** Land 1–2 Tier 1 clubs on a free 30-day pilot, over-deliver, and walk away with the proof assets needed to sign the next club without a free trial.

1. **Pilot offer:** Free 30-day on-site detailing pilot for members, 1–2 designated event/play days per week, no cost to the club or to participating members. Positioned as a member-experience trial, not a vendor pitch.
2. **Over-deliver operationally:** Show up early, staff appropriately for volume, hit every promised turnaround window inside the 4–4.5 hr play window without exception. First impressions with club staff (bag drop, pro shop) matter as much as with members — they're the ones who'll vouch for us internally.
3. **Capture proof assets throughout the pilot:**
   - Written and video testimonials from members and from the GM/golf pro.
   - Before/after photos of vehicles (with member permission).
   - Member emails and opt-ins collected at point of service for a "founding members" list.
   - Utilization data: cars serviced per day, no-show/cancel rate, member satisfaction score.
4. **Close the pilot into a paid agreement** before it lapses — bring a one-page results summary (cars serviced, satisfaction quotes, revenue-share or membership-plan economics) to the renewal conversation.
5. **Use the pilot as the pitch deck for club #2:** testimonials, photos, and utilization numbers become the core of the Tier 1/Tier 2 outreach materials going forward — this is what separates cold outreach #1 from every outreach after it.

## 4. Outreach Sequence

### Identifying the decision-maker
Target, in order of typical authority over member amenities:
1. **General Manager** — final sign-off on any club-level partnership.
2. **Membership Director / Member Experience Director** — often the actual champion; owns the "why would members love this" case.
3. **Head Golf Professional** — controls bag-drop logistics and daily operations; critical operational ally even when not the final decision-maker.

Identify names via the club's website staff page, LinkedIn, and a call to the pro shop ("Who should I speak with about a member amenity partnership?"). At Invited/ClubCorp properties, also identify the regional VP of operations — that relationship can outrank any single GM conversation.

### 4-Touch Cadence

| Touch | Format | Purpose | Assets needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Intro email | Email to GM/Membership Director | Introduce concept in 3-4 sentences, request 15-min call | One-pager PDF (concept, logistics, no-cost-to-club framing) |
| 2. Follow-up with proposal | Email, 5-7 days later | Attach a concrete pilot proposal | 1-page pilot proposal (scope, dates, staffing, insurance/liability note, references if available) |
| 3. Call | Phone, 3-5 days after touch 2 | Answer logistics questions, get a walkthrough scheduled | Talking points sheet; pricing/membership-plan sheet; insurance/COI ready to send |
| 4. On-site walkthrough | In-person, 20 min | See bag drop/parking flow, meet golf ops staff, close on pilot dates | Physical/laptop demo of booking flow, sample of vehicle-tag/sign-up card, references or (post-pilot) testimonial sheet |

Keep every touch short — GMs are busy operators, not marketing audiences. Lead with logistics and zero-risk framing (free pilot, no cost, no liability exposure) every time.

## 5. Member Acquisition Inside a Signed Club

Once a club is signed, conversion to individual members is a separate, deliberate motion:

- **Bag-drop sign-up cards:** Simple QR-code card at the bag drop — "Play clean, come home clean. Scan to book." Staff hands it out or displays it at the drop point.
- **Pro-shop counter card:** Small branded card/stand at the pro-shop counter reinforcing the offer, visible during check-in.
- **"Founding Members" launch offer:** First 25-50 members at a club get a locked-in discounted rate or a free upgrade (e.g., interior + exterior for the price of exterior) for signing up in the first 30 days — creates urgency and rewards early adopters, who then become word-of-mouth engines.
- **Referral incentive:** Existing member refers a fellow member → both get a free or discounted service credit. Word-of-mouth inside a club travels fast; make it worth spreading.
- **Club newsletter/app feature:** Ask the Membership Director to feature the service in the member newsletter, app push, or weekly member email — this is the single highest-leverage acquisition channel once inside a club, since it's a trusted internal endorsement rather than outside advertising. Request this explicitly at signing, not as an afterthought.

## 6. 90-Day Sequencing

**Month 1 — Build**
- Finalize target list (Tier 1, verified names/contacts) and outreach assets (one-pager, pilot proposal template, pricing sheet).
- Begin Touch 1-2 outreach to 6-8 Tier 1 clubs, prioritizing the Invited/ClubCorp portfolio relationship and 2-3 independent family clubs (Gleneagles, Timarron, Stonebriar).
- Secure first on-site walkthroughs; aim to lock in 1-2 pilot commitments by end of month.

**Month 2 — Pilot Live**
- Run the 30-day free pilot at 1-2 founding clubs.
- Operate flawlessly; collect testimonials, photos, member emails, and utilization data throughout.
- Continue Touch 1-3 outreach to remaining Tier 1 list and begin scoping Tier 2 targets in parallel.

**Month 3 — Convert & Expand**
- Convert founding club(s) from pilot to paid partnership.
- Launch member-acquisition motion inside the signed club (bag-drop cards, founding-members offer, newsletter feature).
- Use pilot proof assets to close club #2 (Tier 1 or early Tier 2), aiming to sign without needing a second free pilot.

## 7. Metrics to Track

**Club pipeline (funnel):**
- Clubs contacted → meetings booked → on-site walkthroughs → pilots started → pilots converted to paid.
- Conversion rate at each funnel stage; time from Touch 1 to signed agreement.

**Member acquisition (per club):**
- Members booked (cumulative and weekly run rate).
- % of active members enrolled in a recurring membership/subscription plan vs. one-off bookings.
- Referral-driven sign-ups as % of total.

**Unit economics:**
- Revenue per club-day (per day the crew is on-site).
- Cars serviced per club-day and average ticket size.
- Utilization rate (cars serviced vs. capacity for the day).
- Cost per acquired member (marketing/incentive spend ÷ new members) once paid acquisition begins.

Track all of the above per club so Tier 1 performance data becomes the evidence base for Tier 2 and, eventually, Tier 3 conversations.
