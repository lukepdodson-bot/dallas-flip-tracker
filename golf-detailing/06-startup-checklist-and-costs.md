# Startup Checklist & Costs — Fairway Detail Co.

*Working document — all costs are 2026 estimates, ballpark figures for planning. Get real quotes before purchasing/binding anything.*

## 1. Goal

Launch a professional, club-credible mobile detailing operation for the lowest cost that still looks and performs like a premium service — no cut corners on turf/water safety or insurance, since those are what get us into clubs in the first place. Scope: **one technician, one rig, one truck**, expandable later. This doc covers everything to physically launch and operate day to day. Luke separately owns the LLC formation, business bank account, bookkeeping, and club-ownership negotiations (noted below wherever relevant).

## 3. Insurance & Compliance

Insurance must be bound under the LLC, so Luke's signature is required once quotes are selected — flagged below.

| Item | Purpose | Est. cost | Who owns it |
|---|---|---:|---|
| General liability insurance | Covers third-party bodily injury/property damage | ~$50–75/mo ($600–900/yr) | Luke (signs policy) |
| Garagekeepers / care-custody-control | Covers damage to a member's vehicle while it's in our care | ~$50–85/mo ($600–1,000/yr) | Luke (signs policy) |
| Commercial auto insurance | Covers the truck for business use (personal auto typically excludes this) | ~$100–200/mo ($1,200–2,400/yr) | Luke (signs policy) |
| Certificate of Insurance (COI), club named as additional insured | Clubs will require this before granting lot access — request one per club | Usually free/included, small broker admin fee ($0–50) | Luke (requests from broker after policies bound) |
| TX sales tax permit | Legally required to collect/remit TX sales tax on detailing services | Free | Luke (tied to LLC/EIN — flagged for legal) |
| City mobile-vendor / water-discharge rules | Some DFW municipalities regulate on-site water use/runoff for mobile services — varies by city | $0–150 depending on city, if applicable | Luke/legal (flagged — confirm per each club's city before pilot) |
| **Est. Year-1 insurance total (lean)** | | **~$2,400–2,900/yr** | |
| **Est. Year-1 insurance total (nicer, higher limits)** | | **~$4,000–4,500/yr** | |

## 7. 90-Day Launch Checklist

- [ ] **(Luke)** Finalize LLC formation and EIN
- [ ] **(Luke)** Open business bank account
- [ ] **(Luke)** Confirm final business name (Fairway Detail Co. or alternative)
- [ ] **(Luke)** Approve this budget (lean vs. nicer path)
- [ ] Design logo & basic brand kit
- [ ] Register domain and build one-page website
- [ ] Set up Google Business Profile
- [ ] Set up Google Voice business line
- [ ] Set up booking tool (Square Appointments / Calendly)
- [ ] Set up payments (Square/Stripe) linked to business bank account
- [ ] Set up invoicing tool
- [ ] Get insurance quotes (GL, garagekeepers, commercial auto) for Luke to review

### Weeks 3–4 — Buy & assemble rig, lock insurance, practice
- [ ] Purchase equipment: pressure washer, water tank, generator, vac, extractor, polisher, water-reclamation mat, chemicals, towels, canopy
- [ ] Assemble truck bed/trailer rig; test full water-reclamation setup dry-run
- [ ] Order uniforms, car magnets, A-frame sign, bag-drop cards
- [ ] **(Luke)** Bind insurance policies and sign
- [ ] Request first sample/blank COI from broker to have on hand
- [ ] **(Luke)** Confirm TX sales tax permit filed
- [ ] Research target club's city mobile-vendor/water-discharge rules; flag findings to Luke/legal
- [ ] Run 2–3 practice details (friends/family vehicles); refine timing and workflow
- [ ] Shoot before/after photos for marketing and portfolio

### Weeks 5–8 — Pilot club, first bookings
- [ ] **(Luke)** Finalize pilot club agreement
- [ ] Deliver signed COI naming pilot club as additional insured
- [ ] Set up on-site presence at pilot club (canopy, signage, bag-drop cards at pro shop)
- [ ] Open booking calendar to members; promote via Google Business Profile, club newsletter/bulletin board
- [ ] Complete first paid member bookings; collect feedback and reviews
- [ ] Track per-job cost and timing data to refine pricing

### Weeks 9–12 — Convert, refine, expand
- [ ] Analyze pilot results (bookings, revenue, feedback, turf/runoff compliance)
- [ ] Refine service menu and pricing from real data
- [ ] Collect member testimonials/reviews (Google, club channels)
- [ ] Prepare pitch materials for a second club using pilot results
- [ ] **(Luke)** Negotiate second club agreement
- [ ] Reorder consumables; evaluate equipment upgrades funded by pilot revenue
