# 01 — Market Validation

**Question:** Is anyone already running a dedicated "detail your car while you golf"
business, especially in Dallas–Fort Worth?

**Short answer:** No dedicated, branded player owns this lane — not in DFW, and not
nationally at scale. The activity that exists is fragmented and ad hoc. That is a
green light.

---

## What actually exists today

**1. Generic mobile detailers that *list* golf courses as one of many "we come to you"
locations.** They'll detail your car at home, the office, or a course — the golf course
is incidental, not the business. This is the whole DFW field: The Car Club of Dallas,
Savvy Mobile Detail, My Detail Guys, Wash Doc, Texas Premier, CarDetailing2Go, etc.
None are built around clubs, none have club partnerships, none market "detail while you
play."

**2. Individual detailers experimenting at clubs, ad hoc.** Detailing forums
(Autopia, AutoGeek) are full of operators who set up a pop-up tent in a club lot for a
tournament or by permission, report it going well, then move on. It's a known *tactic*,
not a *company*. Repeated theme in those threads: get club management's permission
first, and don't damage the turf.

**3. Valet companies serving clubs** (Daley Valet, FC Parking, Elite Parking) — parking
and cart staging, not detailing. Adjacent, not competing. Potential channel partners.

**4. The Florida analog you mentioned** is real: private clubs commonly run **shoe-shine
/ shoe-care** as an in-house member amenity (folded into dues). It proves the core
insight — *members will happily hand off a chore to be done while they play* — but it's
about shoes, done in-house, not cars.

**5. Closest "branded" thing** is Club Clean Pros® — but that's a turnkey business for
cleaning **golf clubs (the equipment)**, not member vehicles. Different product.

---

## Why the lane is open (and stays defensible)

- **No category owner.** Nobody has planted a flag as *the* golf-club car care brand.
  First mover with real club relationships can lock exclusivity.
- **The timing insight is the moat.** Mobile detailing's biggest friction is the
  customer waiting around or handing off their car mid-day. A golf round is a
  **guaranteed, predictable 4–4.5 hour window** where the car sits idle and the owner is
  happily occupied. The use case removes detailing's #1 friction. Almost nobody has
  built around this on purpose.
- **The customer is pre-qualified and concentrated.** A private-club parking lot is
  dozens-to-hundreds of high-income owners of nice vehicles, in one place, on a
  schedule. You don't hunt for customers — the club aggregates them for you.
- **Club exclusivity = a durable barrier.** Once a club signs us as its member amenity,
  a second detailer can't just show up. Relationships, not technology, are the moat —
  which is exactly why moving first matters.

## Risks / what to respect

- **Turf & environment.** Wash runoff (brake dust + chemicals) can damage expensive
  turf and violate stormwater rules. We must use water-reclamation mats and
  turf-safe/pH-neutral products. This is also a *sales point* to grounds crews.
- **Brand-fit anxiety.** Some GMs worry a detailer in the lot looks "off the street."
  Answer: uniformed techs, a clean branded rig, insurance certificate naming the club,
  and positioning as *their* premium amenity — not a random vendor.
- **Permission-gated.** You cannot operate without club sign-off. That's a barrier for
  us now and a moat for us later.

## Verdict

**Proceed.** Validated demand signal (shoe-care analog + detailer forum success),
concentrated pre-qualified customers, a genuine timing insight nobody has productized,
and a partnership structure that becomes a competitive moat. Build the DFW footprint
before someone else names the category.
